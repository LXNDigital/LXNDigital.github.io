Disruption Mitigation Workspace

A data-driven workspace for understanding disruption consequence, identifying decision-critical information and restoring continuity with greater confidence.

Global supply chains are no longer disrupted by isolated events. A port closure, regulatory change, cyber incident, supplier constraint or logistics exception can quickly affect production, evidence, obligations, customer commitments and revenue. The challenge is not simply knowing that something has happened. The challenge is knowing what it touches, what decision is required, who must act, and what continuity outcome must be protected.

The workspace starts when something has changed. It helps response teams establish what the change touches, what is known, what is missing or conflicting, what decision is required and what must happen operationally.

How to use the workspace

Use this workspace to move from a changed condition to lifecycle exposure, decision requirements, operational response and continuity outcome.

  1. 1
    Choose the disruption

    Select the changed condition that best matches what has happened. The disruption selection also shows relevant lifecycle entry points, helping you identify the most specific reliable identifier or known condition from which to begin the analysis.

  2. 2
    Start from a lifecycle entry point

    Use the selected entry point to enter the connected lifecycle and trace what the disruption touches across materials, products, movements, evidence, obligations and commitments.

  3. 3
    Work through the six stages

    Move from disruption, to Lifecycle Continuity context, other required data, decision requirements, operational response and continuity outcome.

  4. 4
    Mark information confidence

    For Lifecycle Continuity data and other required data, indicate whether each item is verified, current, assumed, missing or conflicting.

  5. 5
    Complete the decision

    Record the decision owner, required approvals and latest useful decision time, using the decision question and criteria to structure the response.

  6. 6
    Generate the response summary

    Use the summary to capture the current disruption state, key uncertainty, decision position and required operational response.

You do not need complete information to use the workspace. Its purpose is to make what is known, uncertain, missing and decision-critical explicit enough to support action.
